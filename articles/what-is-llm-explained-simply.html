What Is an LLM? Large Language Models Explained in Plain English

No jargon, no hype — just a clear explanation of what large language models are, how they work, and what they can't do.

A
AdyOct 14, 2026 · 5 min read
W

A large language model (LLM) is an AI trained on enormous amounts of text to predict the most likely next word in a sequence. That simple-sounding trick, scaled up with billions of parameters, produces something remarkable: a system that can write essays, code, translate languages, and hold conversations.

The key thing to understand is that LLMs don't "know" facts the way you do — they generate plausible-sounding text based on patterns. That's why they sometimes hallucinate: confidently stating things that aren't true. They're prediction engines, not databases.

💡 Why it matters

Almost every AI product you hear about — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude — is built on an LLM. Our take: understanding this one idea (prediction, not knowledge) makes you instantly smarter about AI's strengths, its failures, and why you should always verify important outputs.
